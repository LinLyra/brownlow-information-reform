# When Judges Get the Data: Recognition Around the 2026 Brownlow Medal Information Reform

## Introduction

Human judges routinely convert multidimensional athletic performance into sparse awards, rankings, and scores. In 2026, Australian Football League field umpires were provided approved player-performance statistics for the first time before finalising Brownlow Medal votes. We examine whether recognition changed unusually around this league-wide information reform and whether any change was concentrated in the newly supplied information.

## Methods

We analyse 47,150 player-match observations from 1,025 matches in 2022–2026. Expected recognition comes from rolling out-of-time models in 2022–2025 and a prospectively frozen 2026 forecast. The primary analysis constructs an equal-weight index of 16 officially supplied statistics using within-match percentile ranks and compares its 2026 voting-sensitivity change with a prespecified index of eight non-supplied performance measures. Regressions include season and position fixed effects and team-result controls, with match-clustered uncertainty. We apply the identical expanding-window specification to pseudo reforms in 2023–2025 and test alternative composites, leave-one-stat-out models, and alignment with independent coach assessments.

## Results

Sensitivity to supplied statistics increased by 0.0301 in 2026 (95% CI 0.0122–0.0480), whereas identical pseudo-reform estimates were −0.0158 in 2023, −0.0221 in 2024, and −0.0034 in 2025. The estimate remained positive under three alternative composite constructions and all 16 leave-one-stat-out specifications (range 0.0219–0.0343). The non-supplied placebo index changed by 0.0045 (95% CI −0.0148–0.0238); however, the direct supplied-minus-placebo contrast remained imprecise at 0.0256 (95% CI −0.0107–0.0618). Umpire votes also became more aligned with independent coach assessments: player-match Spearman correlation increased from a 2022–2025 mean of 0.552 to 0.617 (change 0.0659, 95% match-bootstrap CI 0.0430–0.0894), while top-three overlap increased by 0.0538 (95% CI 0.0188–0.0874). The frozen expected-recognition model achieved 2026 RMSE 0.3005 and materially outperformed transparent benchmarks.

## Conclusion

Brownlow voting changed unusually following the 2026 information reform, becoming more sensitive to the statistics supplied to umpires and more aligned with independent coach assessment. Because supplied and non-supplied performance measures are highly correlated and only one post-reform season is observed, the results do not establish that the reform caused the change. They instead show how prospective prediction can provide a disciplined measurement framework for studying information-assisted human evaluation in elite sport.
